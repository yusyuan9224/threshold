# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the evidence, not the person.

## What this project expects

- **Disagree technically, not personally.** "This measurement doesn't support that conclusion" is
  the kind of pushback this project is built on. "You clearly don't understand Bluetooth" is not.
- **Be honest about what you actually measured.** Saying "I didn't test that" costs nothing here;
  it is the whole reason spike documents are allowed to say `PARTIAL` and `NOT RUN`. Presenting an
  assumption as a result is the one technical behaviour treated as a conduct problem, because
  everything downstream — the supported-device list, the security claims — is only as trustworthy
  as the evidence under it.
- **Accept that maintainer time is finite.** The roadmap is maintainer-directed. A proposal being
  declined, or an issue sitting unanswered for a while, is not a slight.
- **Assume good faith in ambiguity**, and assume the other person may be writing in their second
  language.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory comments, in issues, pull requests, commits, or anywhere else
  this project is discussed.
- Discrimination or exclusionary behaviour based on age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex
  characteristics, gender identity or expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic
  status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing other people's private information — physical or email address, and in this project
  specifically: device identifiers, hostnames, usernames, or Apple IDs, whether someone else's or
  your own — without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions without new evidence, or
  demanding unpaid maintainer attention.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and the
project's documentation — and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a concern privately to the maintainer, [@yusyuan9224](https://github.com/yusyuan9224), by
opening a GitHub conversation with them directly. Do not report conduct concerns as public issues.

Reports are handled privately. The reporter's identity is not disclosed to the person reported
about. If the maintainer is the subject of the report, GitHub's own
[reporting channels](https://docs.github.com/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
apply.

**A security vulnerability is not a conduct report** — those go through
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) and GitHub Security Advisories.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides what response fits, and may: ask someone to change their behaviour; edit or
delete the offending content; or block a person from the repository, temporarily or permanently.
Enforcement decisions are explained to the person affected where doing so does not compromise a
reporter's privacy.
